# Designing Retrieval Practice

## Encoding

Encoding is the act of forming a durable memory trace from incoming information.
Attention acts like a gate: details that receive deliberate processing are more likely to enter the trace.
The goal is not to copy every sentence, but to connect a new idea to an existing question.

## Retrieval

Retrieval practice asks the learner to reconstruct an idea before seeing the answer.
The effort of recall can strengthen later access even when the first attempt is incomplete.
Feedback should follow the attempt, because it repairs the gap without replacing the effort.

## Spacing

Spacing distributes study episodes across time instead of crowding them into one long block.
A short return after a delay can reveal what was understood and what faded.
Spacing and retrieval work together when each return begins with a question rather than a reread.
